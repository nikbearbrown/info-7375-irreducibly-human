**Predict → Build It → Use It → Ship It → Verify**

Bundle [Lesson 3](../../lessons/03-offloading-and-learning/README.md) into one submission. Lesson Assessments are ungraded practice.

## Predict

Which part of a small calculation can you explain unaided before and after AI assistance? Predict your transfer performance. Preserve the original before receiving output or critique.

## Build It

Implement a Python worked-example checker for a chosen arithmetic or text-processing mechanism. Solve one example yourself, then create a new transfer case with an independently calculated expected result.

## Use It

Use Claude to explain a confusing step and help revise your implementation. Attempt the transfer case before asking for its solution. Keep first attempts and assistance in the record.

## Ship It

Submit first attempt, assisted revision, transfer case, and learning note. Include the Python implementation, tests, inputs, evidence, README, PREDICTIONS.md, CONTRIBUTIONS.md, FRICTIONAL.md, and VERIFICATION.md. Identify source material and real assistance. The final version goes to the designated GitHub location and Canvas.

## Verify

Compare your transfer explanation with actual execution. A single exercise is not proof of long-term skill gain or loss. Do not invent struggle if the work was straightforward.

Check the shipped candidate, record actual outcomes, and explain the bounded conclusion. Do not fabricate human participants, permissions, findings, or understanding. If a learning exercise uses a simulation, label it throughout.

## Division of labor

**AI should** explain syntax, give hints, and check calculations after the attempt.

**Human should** attempt the mechanism, explain the transfer case, and report actual learning.
